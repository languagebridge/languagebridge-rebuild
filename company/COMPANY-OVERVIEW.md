# LanguageBridge — Company Overview

**Grade-level content, in every student's language, out loud.**

## The problem

Roughly 1 in 10 U.S. public-school students is an English learner, and the fastest-growing groups — refugee and immigrant students, including those with limited or interrupted formal education — speak languages that mainstream tools serve poorly: Dari, Pashto, Somali, Burmese, Ukrainian, and more. Google Translate gives a literal word swap with no academic context. A teacher with five newcomers speaking four languages has no way to explain "photosynthesis" or run a two-way conversation without pulling an interpreter.

## What LanguageBridge does

A student highlights any word on any page and, in one action, gets:

1. a **plain-English bridge scaffold** ("photosynthesis → light-feeding") that teaches the concept, not just the word,
2. the **cognate in their home-language script**, and
3. **native-speaker audio**.

Plus an **Academic Glossary** of K-12 vocabulary by subject and grade band, and **Talk to Teacher** — a two-way voice translator so a student and teacher can actually talk, on one device, with no interpreter in the room. Eighteen languages, all delivered through a Chrome extension that works on any Chromebook.

## Why we win

| Competitor | What they do | Why we're different |
|---|---|---|
| Google Translate | Literal translation | No academic bridge scaffolds, no student-voice quality loop |
| MS Immersive Reader | Read-aloud + translation | English-centric; weak on underserved languages |
| Ellevation | ELL progress tracking | Roster/reporting, not real-time content comprehension |
| TransACT | Translated school documents | Static PDFs, not interactive, no audio |

Our moat is the **Lexicon flywheel**: teachers and students flag a wrong meaning, wrong sound or misheard word → paid native-speaker linguists correct it → two independent reviewers approve → the corrected entry is served ahead of Azure for every school using that language. The result is a human-verified classroom lexicon for low-resource languages, tagged by subject and grade band, that no off-the-shelf dataset provides. See `flywheel/SPEC.md`.

## Who it's for

K-12 districts and schools serving English learners — bought by EL/multilingual-program directors, curriculum leaders, and IT, and funded through Title III, ESSER successors, and EL/newcomer program budgets.

## Built for schools' rules

Pseudonymous by design (students never create accounts, no PII collected), FERPA / COPPA / Ohio SB 29-aligned, NIST CSF v1.1 controls, and a ready Student Data Privacy Agreement on the ETLA Ohio NDPA V1 standard — the same template districts already sign. One district (Parma City Schools) has signed an MVP DPA.

## The ask / the goal

To reach **thousands of schools and districts** by being the one tool that makes grade-level content accessible to the students the system serves worst — priced so a building can say yes out of existing EL budgets (see [PRICING-AND-PACKAGING.md](PRICING-AND-PACKAGING.md)).
